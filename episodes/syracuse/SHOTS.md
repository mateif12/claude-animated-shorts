# SHOTS — "The One Guy Who Made Rome Look Stupid"

1080×1920 composition, rendered 1440×2560 (see README for the render note), 30 fps, **1990 frames = 66.3 s**.
Voice 0.0–64.5 s, then a 1.8 s silent visual tag. Every reaction lands 0–3 frames before its word (`voice/words.json`).

## Cast (flat bean-head cards, plum ink, `toon.ts`)
| Character | Look | Running jokes |
|---|---|---|
| **Archimedes** | Bald dome with white side tufts, big white beard, white chiton with a blue himation over one shoulder, holds a compass. Usually seated. | **Never looks up.** Calm "smile_closed" bliss while drawing circles in a sand tray, whatever chaos is happening. His only "attack" is a lazy foot on a lever, still not looking. |
| **Marcellus** | Stout general, bronze cuirass, red cloak, helmet with a huge red transverse plume. | **The plume is his dignity.** Full and proud at the start, it wilts a notch after every failure, perks up when he's hopeful (sambuca), and flops flat at the end. It springs up on "by doing math", then wilts one last time in the tag. |
| **Lucius** (fictional soldier) | Skinny legionary: steel helmet with a small red crest, red tunic, grey armour bands, big red rectangular shield. | **Suspicious of everything.** He's the one dangling from the lifted ship, the one whose shield becomes a pincushion, the first to flee from a stick, the stone counter, and the victim of the final callback. |

## Comedy review (before rendering)
1. **First 3 s:** the hook opens on the scale of the fleet. At 2.7 s it cuts to a warship hanging vertically with a man dangling, which is absurd and immediately readable.
2. **Archimedes:** never looks up. That trait is consistent, simple and visual.
3. **Marcellus:** the plume barometer gives him a readable arc with no dialogue.
4. **Jokes that work without narration:** hanging ship, pincushion shield, plume wilting and boinging, helmet poured out like a cup with a fish, an army fleeing a stick, the final callback.
5. **Escalation:** claw → boulders → bigger boulders → arrows from the wall itself → the secret weapon crushed → the general reduced to pouring out his helmet → an army scared of sticks.
6. **Ending:** Rome wins by doing math, and the last image says it never really got over it.

Cut during review: an early idea to end on Marcellus climbing the ladder. He did not lead the climb, so the tag went to the fictional soldier instead (see RESEARCH.md).
