## Facts and sources
- Hannibal, in exile, served King Prusias I of Bithynia in his war against King Eumenes II of Pergamon (c. 184 BC;
  the year is a modern estimate and is not said in the narration).
- Nepos, *Hannibal* 10.2: Eumenes was stronger "on both" land and sea because of his alliance with Rome
  ("utrobique Eumenes plus Romanorum societate valebat").
- Nepos, *Hannibal* 10–11: Hannibal had snakes ("venenatas serpentes") collected and put into clay pots; sent a
  messenger in a small boat with a herald's staff and a letter; the enemy took it for a peace offer and led him to
  Eumenes; the letter contained only mockery of the king; its purpose was to reveal which ship the king was on.
  All Hannibal's ships attacked the king's ship; the pots were thrown at the other Pergamene ships; at first the pots
  made the enemy laugh ("initio risum pugnantibus concitarunt"), then, seeing their decks full of snakes, they fled
  to their naval camp; Eumenes escaped to guards on the shore.
- Venom is disputed: Burton 2021, "Bithynian snake bombs", *Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment*
  (doi:10.1002/fee.2332) reports one herpetologist doubting that so many venomous snakes could be collected and
  another suggesting harmless dice snakes.
- Visual inventions (not claims): the letter's doodle (the real wording is unknown), the elephant cameo, gull, grapes,
  bow tie, Roman thumbs-up, the arrow and the failed wink.

## Script (as recorded)
1. Hannibal once won a sea battle by throwing pots full of snakes at the enemy.
2. Yes, that Hannibal, the elephant guy.
3. By now, he's old, exiled, and running Bithynia's navy, at war with Pergamon.
4. And Pergamon is stronger on land, stronger at sea, and best friends with Rome.
5. So Hannibal sends his men on a very unusual shopping trip.
6. Snakes, lots of snakes, stuffed into clay pots.
7. Before the battle, he sends a messenger over with a letter for the enemy king.
8. They think it's a peace offer and escort him straight to the king's ship.
9. The king opens it. It's just insults.
10. But now Hannibal's fleet knows exactly which ship the king is on.
11. And every ship goes for that one.
12. Then Hannibal's sailors start lobbing pots at the rest.
13. The enemy laughs. Who fights with pottery?
14. Then the pots break.
15. The Roman account says venomous. Modern experts suspect mostly harmless.
16. The sailors did not stay to check.
17. The enemy fleet turns and runs, and the king barely makes it to shore.
18. That letter was never meant to be read. It was a name tag.
