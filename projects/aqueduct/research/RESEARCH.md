# How the Romans built aqueducts: research notes and sources

Research date: 2026-11-04. Every fact used in the narration is listed here with its source.
**Status labels:** VERIFIED = stated in the cited source. ESTIMATE = the source itself gives it as an estimate. NOT USED = could not be confirmed, so it is kept out of the script.

## Facts used in the narration
| # | Claim in the script | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No pumps: Roman aqueducts moved water by gravity alone, along a continuous downhill gradient | VERIFIED | Wikipedia, "Roman aqueduct"; Wikipedia, "Pont du Gard" |
| 2 | Imperial Rome had roughly a million people | ESTIMATE (the source gives about 1,000,000) | Wikipedia, "Roman aqueduct", supply section |
| 3 | The springs were far from the city. Aqua Claudia: about 69 km long, mostly underground, fed from springs near the 38th milestone of the Via Sublacensis | VERIFIED | Wikipedia, "Aqua Claudia" |

| 5 | The Nîmes aqueduct falls about 17 m over about 50 km, roughly 34 cm per km. Vitruvius recommended a gradient of at least 1 in 4,800 | VERIFIED | Wikipedia, "Pont du Gard"; Wikipedia, "Roman aqueduct" |
| 6 | Most of the route was underground. Of Rome's 780–800 km of conduits, about 47 km ran above ground on masonry | VERIFIED | Wikipedia, "Roman aqueduct" |
| 7 | Tunnels were dug from a line of vertical shafts and joined underground. The shafts also served for ventilation, spoil removal and maintenance | VERIFIED (general method) | World History Encyclopedia, "Roman Tunnels" |

## Checked but NOT USED
- Shaft spacing: sources disagree. One gives 35.5 m attributed to Vitruvius, another 30–60 m in practice, and a commonly quoted "every 240 feet, Frontinus" figure could not be found. The script mentions no spacing.
- Frontinus's line about the "idle Pyramids" could not be checked against a primary text (Wikiquote and LacusCurtius were unreachable from this environment), so it is not quoted.
- Inverted siphons are real (lead pipes, for example at Lyon), but they are left out for time. The script never says that arches were the only way to cross a valley; it says arches were used *where* a valley dropped away.

## Visual accuracy notes (what the animation shows, and why)
- **Aqueduct bridge:** three tiers in the manner of the Pont du Gard, with a large lower arcade, a medium middle one and many small upper arches carrying the channel.
- **Artistic licence (deliberate):** real channels were normally roofed with slabs or vaults. The film leaves the channel open so the water can be seen. The water front in shot 10 also travels in seconds; the narration gives the real transit time (about a day, 27 h at Nîmes).
- **Clothing:** workers wear short belted wool tunics, mostly undyed or earth tones. Engineers and surveyors wear an off-white tunic with a coloured cloak. No togas on site, and no anachronistic hats, gloves or helmets.
- **Tools:** groma (vertical staff with a cross and four plumb lines), chorobates (long wooden table on legs), treadwheel crane with wooden jib and rope, wooden arch centering (formwork), iron chisels and mallets.
- **Not shown:** pumps, metal scaffolding, wheelbarrows (not attested for Roman sites), or modern surveying tripods with optics.
