## Points deliberately NOT asserted
- No mechanism for how the Persians got through the gates (betrayal, opened gate, negotiated surrender) is presented
  as fact. The film shows the gate already open / soldiers walking in, with no gate-opening agent.
- The river story is labelled as Herodotus's account in the narration ("In his version…", "the contemporary record never mentions…").
- Scholarly nuance (not narrated, kept here): Gauthier Tolini proposed, from a receipt for repair work on the Enlil Gate,
  that part of the army under Ugbaru forced an entry on the west side and then crossed the river — possibly the seed of the
  riverbed story (WP *Fall of Babylon* [V]). The Chronicle and the Cyrus Cylinder are also post-conquest texts with a
  pro-Cyrus slant (WP *Fall of Babylon* [V]). Hence the open closing question rather than a verdict.
- The commander who entered first was Ugbaru/Gubaru (Gobryas), not Cyrus; the film shows Cyrus's own entry only in the
  "seventeen days later" / twist beat.
- "The greatest city in the ancient world" is rhetorical framing from the brief (Babylon was among the largest cities
  of its time), not a measured claim.

## Visual research (stylised, not reconstruction)
- Walls: baked/mud brick in tan-ochre; crenellated towers; the Ishtar Gate is the blue-glazed exception (lions on the
  Processional Way; dragons and aurochs on the gate). The film keeps the city walls ochre and the gate blue.
- Etemenanki ziggurat: stepped tiers with a temple on top (shown stylised).
- Persian dress: trousers, tunics and soft felt caps for soldiers (Median-style); a crowned king in a long purple robe.
  Babylonian guard: conical helmet, beard, spear. All stylised to the series' bean-head character system.
